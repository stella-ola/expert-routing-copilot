# Research and Validation Plan

## Current evidence status

The existing specification and prototype are concept artifacts. No real customer, specialist, queue, or company data has been used. The following are assumptions until validated:

- customers are frequently misrouted or escalated too late;
- repeated intake is a significant contributor to resolution time;
- the three-category taxonomy captures enough of the support domain;
- the proposed handoff fields are useful and safe;
- the risk weights and confidence bands reflect real error costs;
- better routing improves satisfaction, retention, or support cost.

## Research questions

### Customers

- What happened before, during, and after a difficult support interaction?
- When did the customer first believe automation could not help?
- What information did they repeat, and what felt unnecessary?
- What explanation and wait expectation would preserve trust?
- Which channels or accessibility accommodations are required?

### Specialists

- What facts are required before work can begin?
- Which cases are commonly misrouted, and how are they corrected?
- What signals indicate immediate harm or customer vulnerability?
- When does a summary help, and when does it create automation bias?
- Which decisions must remain human or deterministic?

### Operations and risk partners

- What are current transfer reasons, queue times, handle times, reopen rates, and P0 incident patterns?
- What capacity exists for triage, expert chat, and callbacks?
- Which policies, regulations, audit records, and retention controls apply?

## Proposed methods

| Method | Participants or source | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semi-structured interviews | 6–8 customers across recent support experiences | Journey themes and problem severity |
| Contextual inquiry | 5–7 fraud, billing, and support specialists | Workflow map and handoff requirements |
| Data review | De-identified transfer and resolution events if approved | Baseline funnel and high-friction slices |
| Card sort | Specialists and operations | Refined intent and severity taxonomy |
| Concept test | Customers and specialists | Comprehension, trust, and usability issues |
| Policy workshop | Product, risk, legal, compliance, privacy, operations | Approved overrides and no-go areas |

Sample sizes are directional discovery assumptions, not statistical guarantees.

## Synthesis and traceability

Maintain an evidence table that links each insight to its source, confidence, product implication, and open question. Separate direct observation, participant report, quantitative evidence, interpretation, and product decision. Update the decision log when evidence changes the policy.

## Validation exit criteria

Advance to shadow mode only when the problem is evidenced, taxonomy and labels are usable, handoff fields are approved, safety rules have accountable owners, representative evaluation data can be obtained lawfully, and queue fallback is operational.
